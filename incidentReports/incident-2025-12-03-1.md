# Incident: 2025-12-03 12-38-00

## Summary

```md

Between the hour of 12pm and 1pm on Wednesday, December 3rd, 2025, 4 users encountered uncaught errors when ordering pizza. The event was triggered by a the introduction of chaos at around 12:35pm. The chaos was introduced in order to fulfill a CS 329 assignment.

The introduced chaos caused our pizza factory to be unable to process orders. The event was detected by our Grafana logs. The team started working on the event by looking into the error and factory logs. This severe incident affected 100% of users.

```

## Detection

```md

This incident was detected when our Grafana IRM log alert was triggered and our on-call DevOps developers were paged. The issue was detected and action was taken within minutes.

```

## Impact

```md

For 4 minutes between 12:38pm and 12:42pm on 12/03/2025, our users were unable to recieve JWT pizzas.

This incident affected 4 customers (100% of current service users), who experienced unexpected 500 errors due to our factory being down.

No support tickets were submitted.

```

## Timeline

```md

All times are UTC.

- _12:38_ - chaos was introduced
- _12:39_ - our head developer was paged and opened our Grafana logs
- _12:41_ - our developer followed a link to end chaos that was included in the factory log
- _14:42_ - chaos was cleared and pizzas could once again be ordered

```

## Response

```md

After receiving a page at 12:39pm, Chance came online in our Grafana dashboard.

This engineer did simple log tracing and quickly found the link to end the chaos.

```

## Root cause

```md

Agreeing to have chaos introduced into our system was the main cause of the outage.

```

## Resolution

```md

This issue was quickly and simply resolved by following a link in our factory logs, which was lablled "follow link to end of chaos".

```

## Prevention

```md

In the future, we will not need to agree to chaos being introduced in order to pass a DevOps class.

```
